Edge Weights as Relationship Strength

· Edges can represent interactions, flows of information or goods,
   similarities/affiliations, or social relations

· Specifically for social relations, a `proxy' for the strength of a tie can be:

    · the frequency of interaction (communication) or the amount of flow (exchange)
    · reciprocity in interaction or flow
    · the type of interaction or flow between the two parties (e.g., intimate or not)
    · other attributes of the nodes or ties (e.g., kin relationships)
    · the structure of the nodes' neighborhood (e.g., many mutual `friends')

· Surveys and interviews can reveal the existence of mutual or one-sided
   strength/affection with greater certainty, but proxies are also useful

Homophily ~ tendency to relate to people with similar characteristics                      Homophily Heterophily
                (status, beliefs, etc.)
                                                                                           Strong TIES Weak
  · tendency to homogeneous groups (clusters) where forming relations is easier
  · extreme homogenization can act counter to innovation and idea generation               Transitivity  Bridging

            => heterophily is thus desirable in some contexts                                       CLUSTERING
  · homophilous ties can be strong or weak
                                                                                           Cliques       Interlinked
Transitivity ~ `if there is a tie between A and B and a tie between B and C,                             groups
                 then A and C will also be connected in a transitive network
                                                                                           Social network
  · strong ties are more often transitive than weak ties; transitivity thus indicates the
       existence of strong ties (but is not a necessary or sufficient condition)

  · transitivity and homophily together lead to the formation of cliques (fully
       connected clusters)

Bridges ~ nodes and edges that connect different groups

  · usually weak ties, but not every weak tie is a bridge
  · facilitate inter-group communication, increase social cohesion, and help spur

       innovation

Shortest path algorithms

B. V. Cherkassky, A. V. Goldberg, T. Radzik: Shortest paths algorithms: Theory and experimental evaluation,
in: Mathematical Programming, 73, 1996, pp. 129-174.

· Single source: Given a graph G=(V,E), with weight function w(u,v) = wu,v , and a

   single source vertex, s, return the shortest paths from s to all other vertices in V.

      · General graphs (~ directed, negative edge weights allowed): Bellman-Ford algorithm with the
          complexity O(|V||E|)

      · Graphs without negative edge weights: Dijkstra´s algorithm with the complexity O(|V|2)
      · To find the shortest path between only two vertices, the algorithm can stop after that shortest path

          is found.

· All pairs: Given a graph G=(V,E), with weight function w(u,v) = wu,v , return the
  shortest path from u to v,  u,v  V.

      · Graphs with negative edge weights allowed: Floyd-Warshall algorithm with the complexity O(|V|3)
      · Sparser graphs with negative edge weights allowed: Johnson´s algorithm with the complexity

          O(|E||V|+|V|2  log2(|V|))
      · Graphs without negative edge weights: Dijkstra´s algorithm with the complexity O(|V|3)

Interpretation of measures

Centrality measure Interpretation in social networks

· Degree       How many people can this person reach directly?
· Betweenness
· Closeness    Network of music collaborations: How many people has he/she collaborated with?
· Eigenvector
               How likely is this person to be on the most direct route in the network?

               Network of spies: Through whom is most of the information likely to flow?

               How fast can this person reach everyone in the network?

               Network of sexual relations: How fast will an STD spread to the rest of the network?

               How well is this person connected to other well-connected people?

               Network of citations: Who is the author cited most by other well-cited authors?

Structural equivalence:

 ~ expresses the similarity between actors in a social
   network

 · is based on the neighbors they share (or, equivalently,
   the number of identical ties they have)

 · two actors are structurally equivalent if their
   positions can be swapped without modifying the
   overall structure of the network

 · approximate structural equivalence can be used for
   hierarchical clustering of social networks

Small Worlds

A small world ~ a network that looks almost random
but exhibits a significantly high clustering coefficient
and a relatively short average path length

 ~ nodes cluster locally and can be reached in a few steps

· very common in social networks because of:

   · transitivity in strong social ties and
   · the ability of weak ties to reach across clusters

· `small world' networks have many clusters but also

many bridges between the clusters that help shorten

the average distance between nodes                            "6 degrees" of separation are well-known

Social connections ­ strong and weak ties

Social media connect users to each other extremely easily

    · users might have thousands of friends online
    x but in practice, connections are not of the same strength and can play different

       roles in community formation and information diffusion

         · strong ties ~ close friends and weak ties ~ acquaintances
         · strength of weak ties (Granovetter, 1973) ­ occasional encounters with distant

             acquaintances can provide important information about new opportunities for job
             search

    · How to estimate the strengths of ties for advanced analysis?

         · analyze network topology
         · learn from user profiles and attributes
         · learn from user activities

´Shortcut´ bridge

Bridges are relatively rare in real-life networks

   · relax the definition by checking if the distance
      between two terminal nodes increases (to more than
      2) if the edge is removed
      ==> the larger the distance, the weaker is the tie

               · (2,5) = 4 if (2,5) is removed
               · (5,6) = 2 if (5,6) is removed
              · (5,6) is a stronger tie than (2,5)

Learning the strength of the ties from profiles and interactions

Twitter (one can follow others without their confirmation)

· the real friendship network is determined by the frequency with which two users talk to each
    other, rather than the follower-followee network

· the real friendship network is more influential in driving Twitter usage

Facebook: strengths of ties can be predicted based on various information, e.g.,

· friend-initiated posts, messages exchanged in wall posts, number of mutual friends, etc.

Learning numeric link strength, e.g., by maximum likelihood estimation

· user profile similarity determines the strength
· link strength, in turn, determines user interaction

     maximize the likelihood based on observed profiles and interactions

Learning the strength of the ties from user activities

· We can learn how the users influence their friends if the user activity
  log is accessible

· Maximize the likelihood of user activity depending on the adopted
  influence modeling method

   · Linear threshold model (LTM)

   · Independent cascading model (ICM)

· Influence modeling is important for understanding information
  diffusion, spread of new ideas, and word-of-mouth (viral) marketing
