Homophily, Social Influence, and Affiliation

Why is homophily often present in a social network?

   Selection

        ~ people tend to choose friends who are like themselves
        · can operate at different scales and levels of intentionality

             · you actively choose friends that are like yourself among a small
               group of people

             · your school's population is relatively homogeneous compared to
               the overall population, so your environment compels you to choose
               friends like yourself

Mutable and Immutable Characteristics:

· Selection operates differently based on the type of characteristic

   · Immutable: Characteristics that don't change (gender, race) or change
      consistently with the population (age, generation)

   · Mutable: Characteristics that can change over time (behaviors, beliefs,
      interests, opinions)

Selection and Social Influence:

· Research has shown that people are susceptible to social influence
  ~ they may change their behaviors so as to more closely resemble
    the behaviors of their friends (surprise!)

· "Bad company corrupts good character." ­ 1 Corinthians 15:33

· Social influence is the opposite of selection.

   · Selection: Individual characteristics drive the formation of links

   · Social influence: Existing links shape people's mutable characteristics

Longitudinal Studies (long time span):

 · Difficult to tell if selection or social influence is at play with
   a single snapshot of a network

 · Longitudinal studies tracking social connections and individual
   behaviors over time can help researchers uncover the effect of
   social influence

 · Does behavior change after changes to network, or does
   network change after changes in behavior?

Example:

· Longitudinal studies have been used to determine if selection or social
   influence has had more an effect on teenagers' scholastic achievement and
   drug use

· Understanding these effects can be helpful in developing interventions

· If drug use displays homophily in a network, a program to target social

Methodology: Measuring Triadic Closure

1. Take two snapshots of the network at times t1 and t2
2. For each k, identify all pairs of nodes who have exactly k friends in

    common at t1 but who are not directly connected by an edge
3. Define T(k) to be the fraction of these pairs that form an edge

    by t2. This is the probability that a link will form between two
    people with k friends in common

4. Plot T(k) as a function of k to illustrate the effect of common
    friends on link formation

Till now, mostly a relation in a network has a positive connotation, but there
exist social networks where also negative relationships can be expressed ­
antagonistic (enemy).

In the following part

   · Positive relations ­ friendly
   · Negative relations ­ enemy

Question: when is the network in a balanced state?

  · We will see how local properties enforce global properties, cf. Schelling Model of Segregation

Note: we will greatly oversimplify ­ e.g., we consider networks where all pairs
of nodes are connected by a positive or negative relation. Later we will relax it.
